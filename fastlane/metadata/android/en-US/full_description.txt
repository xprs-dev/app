XPRS is a messenger and app launcher that works off the grid. It talks over the internet, over a Reticulum overlay, and straight over Bluetooth LE, and it stitches those together so a message or a file gets to the other side over whatever path happens to be up. If there is no internet, it falls back to a Bluetooth street mesh and long range radio. If there is internet, it uses it.

There are no accounts, no phone number and no server in the middle. Your identity is a key the device makes itself.

<b>What it does</b>

<ul>
<li><b>Many transports at once.</b> A message can go out over the internet, over Bluetooth LE, or over Reticulum, and whichever copy arrives first wins.</li>
<li><b>It keeps working with no internet.</b> A Bluetooth street mesh passes messages from phone to phone with nothing in between, and cheap LoRa stations push that reach out over kilometres.</li>
<li><b>Store and forward.</b> A message for somebody who is away is carried by the stations in between and delivered when they come back.</li>
<li><b>Chat.</b> One-to-one conversations, closed groups and a local room, with pictures and files.</li>
<li><b>Social.</b> Posts, replies and follows, spread over the same transports.</li>
<li><b>Torrents and shared folders.</b> Files move peer to peer, from whoever has them.</li>
<li><b>Wapps.</b> Small WebAssembly apps that draw through one shared native interface and use the host's transports: chat, social, mail, a media player, a file browser and more. You can install others, or write your own in the built-in editor.</li>
</ul>

<b>Network use</b>

A fresh install connects to a few public Reticulum hubs, which you can change or switch off in Settings. Maps (OpenStreetMap), torrents, and pictures you send to somebody on another network reach the internet only when you use them. NOSTR and I2P are off unless you turn them on.
